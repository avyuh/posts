Read this before using the page

The Pink Line is not open. As of 5 September 2026 no section carries passengers. The elevated stretch (Kalena Agrahara–Tavarekere, 6 stations, Bannerghatta Road) is built and awaiting a Government of India inauguration date, targeted within 2026. The underground stretch (Dairy Circle–Nagawara, 12 stations) is targeted at mid-2027 on BMRCL's own working estimate. This line has passed through eleven announced opening dates since 2023, so treat both targets as intentions, not schedules. Buying here is a bet on delivery.

“Pink adds” is the honest measure: first metro means no operational station within 3 km today; closer means Pink beats the current station by at least 1 km; already served means the project has metro now and Pink adds an interchange, not access. Station distances are straight-line to mapped future station points — BMRCL publishes no platform coordinates, so treat them as approximate.

The metro upside already appears to be in the price. Comparable Bengaluru complexes that already have an operating metro station trade at roughly ₹12,200–₹14,500 per sq ft. The Tier 1 median on this corridor is about ₹12,600. There is no measurable buy-ahead discount here: you would pay a connected-property price today for connectivity arriving in months on the Bannerghatta stretch, or years at Nagawara. That does not make these bad homes — it means the metro is a reason to live here, not a reason to expect a repricing.

The middle of the line has no stock, and that is a finding. Across the eight central stations (Dairy Circle to Cantonment) and the four north-inner stations (Pottery Town to Kadugondanahalli), 71 candidate buildings were examined and none met the family-play bar — nor a relaxed one. Those wards are dense plotted land, heritage bungalow zones and low-rise stock. Central Pink is a commute asset for people who already live there, not a place to buy a family apartment.
